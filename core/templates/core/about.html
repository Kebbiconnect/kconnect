About Kebbi Progressive Youth Network

A modern civic network, community media, advocacy, development, and opportunities platform for the people of Kebbi State.

Our Motto

"One Voice, One Change."

The Kebbi Progressive Youth Network (KPN) is a youth-driven, state-wide organization dedicated to building unity, transparency, and sustainable development across all 21 Local Government Areas of Kebbi State.

We believe in collective action, civic awareness, and grassroots empowerment as the ultimate tools to move our communities forward and ensure the voices of our people count in governance and decision-making. Through our extensive network of leaders down to the Ward level, we bridge the gap between the government and the governed.

Our Vision

To build a progressive, transparent, and united Kebbi State where every citizen has equal opportunities for growth, development, and meaningful participation in governance and societal advancement.

Our Mission

To build a strong, connected civic network across Kebbi State—a platform where our collective voice influences the future of our state. We mobilize and empower citizens through advocacy, education, and community engagement.

The Five Pillars of KPN

Our work is categorized into five core functions that drive progress across the state.

KPN MEDIA

The KPN Newsroom provides verified, grassroots reporting, community alerts, and development news from all 225 wards in Kebbi State.

KPN CIVIC

Promoting good governance, voter education, and holding leadership accountable at the local, zonal, and state levels.

KPN COMMUNITY

Welfare programs, charity drives, and direct community interventions designed to support the vulnerable and build local resilience.

KPN OPPORTUNITIES

A centralized hub connecting the youth of Kebbi State to scholarships, grants, jobs, and empowerment fellowships.

KPN ADVOCACY

Championing policy changes, infrastructural development, and social justice campaigns that directly impact the lives of Kebbi citizens.

KPN Media: How Our Newsroom Works for You

KPN Media is our newsroom, built on one idea: a story should not disappear after publication day. Here is how we follow stories, show our evidence, and keep the public record open to everyone.

Living Stories

Important stories do not end on the day they are published. KPN keeps each one as a living record: the original report, every later development added in order, and a permanent Story ID printed on the article so you can always find its full history.

Public Promise Tracker

When leaders and public institutions make promises, KPN records them and keeps checking: what was promised, what evidence exists, and whether it has been delivered. Follow every tracked promise on the Promise Tracker.

Future Signal

Careful analysis of changes taking shape in Kebbi — new trends, risks and opportunities, and the evidence behind them. Future Signal pieces are clearly labelled as analysis. They are not reports of established fact, and they are not predictions.

Community Radar

People across our 225 wards see things first. Community reports bring local problems and achievements to the newsroom, where editors review them before anything is treated as news. A reporter’s private details are never published.

Truth Layers

Not everything reported is equally certain. On important stories, KPN shows what is confirmed, what is only an official statement, what is still developing, and what sources dispute — so you can see exactly how sure we are, and why.

Human Impact Files

Behind every policy and project are people. Human Impact Files tell their stories with their knowledge and consent, protecting their privacy and dignity — including the choice to remain anonymous.

KPN Knowledge Archive

Every published story, update and tracked promise becomes part of a permanent public record. In the KPN Knowledge Archive you can search the whole record — by topic, place, institution, or a story’s permanent Story ID — and revisit how events developed over time.

How KPN checks what it publishes

Before KPN calls anything confirmed, editors check it against evidence and show the source on the article itself. Where we rely on an official statement, we say so plainly — an official statement is not the same as a confirmed fact. And when we get something wrong, we publish a correction openly instead of quietly changing the story.
